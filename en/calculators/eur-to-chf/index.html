How not to overpay abroad

The rate above is the mid-market rate. As a private person you almost never get exactly that, because banks, card providers and exchange offices earn from the difference. Three things make the biggest difference in practice:

  • Pay in the local currency. If a card terminal or ATM asks whether to charge you in euros, choose the local currency. The instant conversion offered (“dynamic currency conversion”) is usually much more expensive.
  • Check your card's fees. Many cards charge a surcharge for payments in foreign currencies. Some cards have no such fee; a look at the price list is worth it before you travel.
  • Do not exchange cash at the airport. Exchange offices at airports and stations often have poor rates. Compare the offered rate with the mid-market rate above: the bigger the gap, the more expensive.

Where the rates come from

The exchange rates come from Yahoo Finance and show the current mid-market rate. They are updated automatically every two hours. Frequently asked questions

How much is 100 Euro in Swiss franc?

That depends on the current exchange rate. The calculator above shows the amount live, the table the most common amounts.

Why does my bank give me less Swiss franc?

The calculator shows the mid-market rate. Banks and exchange offices add a markup and sometimes fixed fees.

How up to date is the rate?

The rates are updated automatically every two hours from Yahoo Finance. The date and time are shown right below the calculator.

Does the calculator cost anything?

No. The currency converter is free, without sign-up and without ads. The calculation runs in your browser.

How to read the figures

For every country you see two reference values: the top rate of income tax and a reference rate for capital income such as gains, dividends or interest. The calculator multiplies your amounts by these rates. That gives an upper estimate, not your actual tax: allowances, progressive brackets, social security, tax treaties and special regimes can change the result a lot.

Residence, 183 days and double taxation

Where you pay tax usually depends on your tax residence, not your passport. Many countries look at a permanent home, your centre of vital interests or around 183 days of presence, but the rules differ from country to country. Leaving one country does not automatically end your tax liability there. Double tax treaties decide which country may tax which income.

For digital nomads

If you move often, write down your days per country. Many tax questions start with: where did you spend how many days, and where is your home? Some countries only tax foreign income when it is brought into the country, others tax worldwide income from the first day of residence.

Frequently asked questions

Where do I have to pay tax as a digital nomad?

Usually where you are tax resident. Many countries use criteria such as a permanent home, centre of vital interests or around 183 days of presence. Without a clear residence, several countries may claim taxes. Get advice for your situation.

Is the figure shown my actual tax?

No. The calculator uses the top income tax rate and a reference rate for capital income. Your actual tax is usually lower because of allowances and progressive brackets.

Which countries have low taxes on capital gains?

Several countries, for example some in the Gulf region or in Asia, tax private capital gains low or not at all. Use the table and the sources linked for each country, and check the rules for your income type.

How current is the data?

Each country shows its check date and sources. The current data was checked on 2026-11-05.
